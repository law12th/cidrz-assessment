# G. The new mandatory reporting field

**For:** senior CIDRZ and Ministry colleagues · **From:** Engineering 

## What is being asked

The Ministry needs a new mandatory reporting field in use at all 1,500 facilities within two weeks.
Engineering estimates six.

## Why six weeks

Three pieces of work, none safely skippable:

1. Every facility holds its own copy of its records, so adding a field means changing 1,500 separate
   databases — including the 300 sites offline for days at a time.
2. Facilities and the national server exchange data in a fixed format. Changing it means older
   clinic software cannot read the new messages, so both versions must work side by side during the
   changeover.
3. The field arrives through a new connection to an outside system, which needs a security review
   before patient information crosses it.

## The risk of compressing it

Skip the compatibility work and clinics offline when the change ships stop sending data until
somebody visits them in person. Skip the security review and we accept an unexamined route into
patient records. Both risks land on clinics, not on engineering, and the second is a legal exposure
for the Ministry.

## The options

| Option | What happens | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Full delivery, six weeks** | Every site, every safeguard | The deadline is missed by four weeks |
| **B. Phased** *(recommended)* | Live in two weeks at the 400 well-connected facilities; the rest by week six | Reporting starts on time for most of the country, but national figures are incomplete for four weeks and must be labelled so |
| **C. Compress to two weeks** | Drop the compatibility work, shorten the security review | Likely loss of contact with offline sites, and an unreviewed interface carrying patient data |

## Recommendation

**Option B.** It starts collecting on the Ministry's date and keeps both safeguards. It asks one
thing in return: that figures for the first four weeks are published as covering 400 facilities, not
1,500, so nobody reads a partial picture as a complete one.

I would not recommend Option C at any price. Its failure is invisible on the day and surfaces weeks
later as clinics quietly out of contact — and the sites it cuts off first are the remote ones the
reporting is meant to reach.
